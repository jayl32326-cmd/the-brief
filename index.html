Step two · where it stops

It matches words. It does not understand you.

Three things the free tool gets wrong

It is fooled by implication. Say "so the support team stops getting these emails" and it sees why. Say "I want fewer support tickets" — the same reason, no marker word — and it sees nothing.

It cannot tell which gap matters. A missing when on a throwaway script is noise. A missing why on a refactor is the whole job. It weights them the same.

It cannot see a contradiction. "Don't change anything in production, just fix the deploy" reads as complete. It is not — it is two instructions that cannot both hold.

Said plainly because you will find all three within a few minutes of using it, and a tool that hides its own limits is not worth trusting with a harder job.

Improve the video production pipeline.

The free tool, today — real

Four circumstances missing

  • Flags where, by what means, why, when as absent.
  • Asks you to supply each one.
  • Treats all four as equally important.
  • Has no idea what "improve" means here.

The AI version — planned

"Improve" is two different jobs

Reading A — fasterCut render time. Touches the encoder settings and the job queue. Reversible.
Reading B — betterRaise output quality. Touches the model and the grade. Changes every published file from here on.
  • These lead to opposite changes. An agent will pick one silently.
  • Also flags a conflict you did not notice: no outcome metric, so "improved" cannot be checked afterwards.
  • Asks one question — faster or better — not four.
  • Separates safe inspection from production changes, and says which it is about to do.

None of the right-hand column exists. It is written out as a concrete illustration of the one capability worth building, so you can tell me whether it is worth building. No AI produced it; I wrote it by hand.

Step four · the only thing I am asking

The free tool stays free and unchanged either way. This is about whether the version that understands the instruction is worth making.

Tell me about the request that went wrong.

There is no form and no mailing list — deliberately. If you found this through a discussion thread, reply there: what you asked, what the agent did instead, and whether seeing the two readings up front would have caught it. A reply in the open is more useful than a private note, because everyone else reading can disagree with it.

The question I actually want answered: when your agent misunderstood you, was the missing piece something this instrument would have shown you? If the answer is no, that is the more useful reply.

No form, no account, no sign-up, no cookie, no analytics, no tracking of any kind on this page. Nothing you paste into the tool is transmitted anywhere — it never leaves your browser, which you can confirm by reading the source or pulling your network cable. There is no address here to harvest and no list to join, because neither exists yet.
